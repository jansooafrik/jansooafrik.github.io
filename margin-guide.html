How to Calculate Your Sanitary Paper Reselling Margin

Your margin is simply the difference between what you pay for stock and what you sell it for, expressed as a percentage. Knowing this number well — for every product you stock — is what separates a reseller who is genuinely growing from one who is just moving boxes.

The basic formula

There are two common ways resellers talk about margin, and it helps to know both because suppliers and customers may use either one:

  • Markup — profit as a percentage of your cost price
  • Margin — profit as a percentage of your selling price

These are not the same number, and mixing them up is one of the most common pricing mistakes new resellers make.

Worked example: Mini Rolls

Bulk cost: KSh 660 for 30 rolls → KSh 22 per roll

Your selling price: KSh 35 per roll

Profit per roll: KSh 35 − KSh 22 = KSh 13

Markup: 13 ÷ 22 × 100 = 59%

Margin: 13 ÷ 35 × 100 = 37%

Notice the markup (59%) looks far more impressive than the margin (37%) — but they describe the exact same sale. Always ask which one a number refers to before comparing it to your own.

Don't forget your hidden costs

The cost price of stock is only part of the picture. Before you decide your true margin, account for:

  • Transport or delivery costs to get stock to your shop
  • Spoilage or damage during storage and handling
  • Time spent sourcing, restocking and managing the business
  • Any credit you extend to customers that isn't repaid on time

A product that looks profitable on paper can quietly lose money once these are factored in. Track them, even roughly, for a month before trusting your margin number.

A practical starting range

For sanitary paper products in Nairobi's retail and reseller market, many resellers work with markups in the 40–70% range depending on the product, location, and how much competition exists nearby. There is no single right number — the right margin is whatever keeps your shelves moving while still leaving you a livable profit after all costs.
