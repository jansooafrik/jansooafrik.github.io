5 Practical Ways to Improve Stock Turnover

Stock turnover is how quickly you sell through what you've bought and restock. Faster turnover means your money spends less time sitting on a shelf as tissue paper and more time working for you as cash. For a small reseller, this often matters more than the size of your margin.

Five practical habits to build

  1. Stock what actually moves, not what looks impressive. It's tempting to keep every product line fully stocked, but a shelf full of slow-moving serviette packs ties up cash that could be restocking your best-selling mini rolls. Track what sells weekly and lean into it.
  2. Order smaller, more frequent batches once you know your pace. Bulk discounts are attractive, but if a large order sits for two months before it sells out, the "savings" are offset by tied-up cash and shelf space. Match your order size to your actual sell-through rate.
  3. Keep a simple written or phone-note log of what sells each week. You don't need software for this. A notebook or a notes app entry — product, quantity sold, date — quickly shows you patterns: which days are busiest, which products run out first, and which are dead weight.
  4. Rotate stock so older items sell first. Place newer deliveries behind existing stock rather than in front. This is a small habit that prevents older rolls from sitting untouched at the back while fresher stock sells first.
  5. Watch for slow movers early, and act on them. If a product hasn't moved in a few weeks, don't wait — consider a small bundle offer, a modest price adjustment, or simply order less of it next time. Recognising a slow mover early protects your cash better than discovering it after a month.

Quick way to think about it: if you turn over your stock twice a month instead of once, the same amount of starting capital effectively works twice as hard for you over that month.

Turnover speed and margin work together — a slightly lower margin on fast-moving stock can outperform a high margin on stock that barely sells.
